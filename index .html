A new way to think about work
The 8 Key Relationships of the Modern Work Experience
The traditional job description is tired and archaic. It lists tasks and qualifications but says nothing about what it actually feels like to work somewhere. This framework reimagines the job description as a holistic portrait of an employee's experience.
Why job descriptions fail candidates and companies
We know that being successful at a company goes far deeper than accomplishing tasks and showing up with degrees or certificates. Yet the reference document we use to attract talent rarely captures any of that depth.

The Work Experience Description changes that. It treats the job posting as a significant opportunity to capture the candidate's mind beyond tasks and qualifications, helping them holistically envision themselves as belonging to the company and embodying the role. When you write to all 8 relationships honestly, the right candidates self-select in. The wrong ones self-select out. Everyone saves time.
The 8 Key Relationships
Relationship 01
The People I Work With
Who I serve, manage, collaborate, partner, and engage with
Work is fundamentally human. Who someone works alongside shapes their daily experience more than almost any other factor. This relationship surfaces the quality and character of those connections.
In your WED: "What your experience will be"
Relationship 02
The Technology I Use
The technology that connects me to my work, workforce, and workplace
Technology is no longer just a tool. It is the medium through which work happens. Whether it is tools for deep focus, platforms for distributed teams, or AI that augments capability, technology shapes identity and experience.
In your WED: "What your experience will be"
Relationship 03
The Places I Work
Where I do my work and the physical space in which work gets done
Place is not just logistics. It is context. The energy of a shared office, the focus of a home environment, the rhythm of a hybrid schedule. Where work happens shapes how work feels.
In your WED: "What your experience will be"
Relationship 04
The Sense of Belonging
The inclusive community my organization creates and my place in the wider world
People need to feel they belong, not just tolerated but genuinely welcomed as themselves. This relationship addresses how the organization creates equity, inclusion, and community.
In your WED: "Why you belong here and how you will grow"
Relationship 05
The Work I Do
My work, my ways of working, the norms and methods I use, and how my work strengthens me
Not just what tasks someone performs, but how that work is done, what standards govern it, and how doing it well makes the person better. Work should strengthen people, not just consume them.
In your WED: "What success looks like" and "What you will bring"
Relationship 06
How Work Affects My Life
My wellbeing, compensation, rewards, and how my worthiness is reflected by the organization
Compensation, benefits, flexibility, and wellbeing are not perks to list at the bottom. They are signals of how the organization views its people's worth. Candidates read them that way. Write them that way.
In your WED: "How this work affects your life"
Relationship 07
The Organization I Work For
The mission, purpose, culture, and leadership behaviors
People want to work for something, not just at something. This relationship surfaces the mission that animates the company, the culture that defines behavior, and the leadership that sets the tone.
In your WED: Company Summary and "Why you belong here"
Relationship 08
How I Grow as a Human
The education, experiences, and exposure that support my growth and give me purpose
The best companies do not just employ people, they develop them. Not just career development opportunities but the specific ways this role will expand someone's capability, identity, and sense of what is possible.
In your WED: "Why you belong here and how you will grow"
